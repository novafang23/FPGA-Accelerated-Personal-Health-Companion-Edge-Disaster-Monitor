3. SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE & INTERCONNECTS

The "Intercom vs. The 4-Lane Walkie-Talkie" Analogy

On the Zynq SoC, the CPU and FPGA communicate over a 32-lane private highway (AXI4-Lite). On our micro-sized ForgeFPGA, we only have 4 pins. We built a 4-lane high-speed walkie-talkie: the ESP32 splits 8-bit sensor bytes into two 4-bit nibbles, strobes the digital doorbell, and the FPGA snaps them together in hardware in under 40 nanoseconds.

Dual-Core FreeRTOS Partitioning on ESP32-S3:
  • Core 0 (The Paramedic): High-speed 50 Hz optical sensor sampling, driving the 4-bit FPGA nibble link, capturing 20 ns IBI timestamps via hardware interrupt, and maintaining rolling HRV (RMSSD).
  • Core 1 (The AI Chief Physician): Environmental sensor acquisition (BME280/PMS5003), running the INT8 TinyML model in <1 µs, executing the deterministic clinical Rule Engine (CTSI/PRSI), and rendering the OLED GUI.

4. THE 7 KILLER ANALOGIES (Judge Defense Cheat-Sheet)

1. The "CEO and the Assembly Line" (Why FPGA Acceleration?)

Think of the CPU as a CEO. If the CEO must manually clean 50 raw optical pulses a second, they burn all their energy and battery. Instead, our FPGA builds a physical automated assembly line that filters noise and extracts pulse intervals in silicon, handing the CEO a finished note: 'Heart rate is 128, IBI is 468 ms.' The CEO is 100% free to run our TinyML model.

2. The "Formula 1 Wind Tunnel vs. The Pocket Commuter" (Why Two FPGAs?)

The Zynq-7000 was our Formula 1 wind tunnel: an industrial testing platform where we proved our Verilog RTL met cycle-accurate timing at 69.45 MHz. But you cannot give a $250 board to a rural laborer. ShrikeFi is our mass-production commuter: running the exact same Verilog filter on a $1.50 micro-FPGA for an ultra-affordable $15 wearable.

3. The "Smart Elevator Scale" (Why is the Filter O(1)?)

To find the average weight of 8 people in an elevator when someone leaves and enters, a naive algorithm weighs all 8 people again. Our $O(1)$ hardware filter remembers the running total, subtracts the person leaving, adds the person entering, and bit-shifts (`>> 3`) in a single 20-nanosecond clock cycle.

4. The "4-Lane Walkie-Talkie" (The 4-Bit FPGA Link)

Transmitting 32-bit IBI cycle counts over 4 physical pins by sending 8 sequential nibbles. At 25 MHz GPIO toggling, an entire packet transfers in 320 nanoseconds—over 3,000× faster than optical sampling!

5. The "Paramedic & The AI Chief Physician" (Dual-Core FreeRTOS)

Core 0 is the paramedic at the patient's side (never drops an optical pulse); Core 1 is the hospital chief physician (runs AI diagnostics and updates the display).

6. The "Teacher and the Student" (Knowledge Distillation)

The clinical Rule Engine is a strict medical professor (100% accurate, but heavy). The INT8 Neural Network is an eager student trained on 50,000 extreme disaster flashcards. The student learned to make the exact same triage decisions with 83.44% accuracy in <1 microsecond.

7. The "Flight Simulator" (Verilog Self-Checking Testbenches)

Before touching physical silicon, we validated our Verilog modules in an automated software flight simulator (`tb_ppg_system.v` and `tb_forgefpga_system.v`), testing pulse waves, refractory periods, and noise rejection.

5. LIKELY JUDGE QUESTIONS & WINNING DEFENSE

Q1: "Why do you have two FPGA architectures in this project?"

Answer: "The Zynq-7000 is our verified architectural reference baseline, proving timing closure at 69.45 MHz and 0 DSP/BRAM usage. The ShrikeFi platform (Renesas ForgeFPGA + ESP32-S3) proves commercial deployability, reducing BOM cost from $250 to under $20 while executing the exact same vendor-agnostic RTL."

Q2: "Why not do all the filtering in software on the ESP32-S3?"

Answer: "Software signal processing on an RTOS suffers from interrupt latency and task jitter when WiFi or sensor interrupts fire. Hardware acceleration guarantees deterministic, jitter-free 20 ns IBI timing while letting the CPU sleep between evaluations."

Q3: "How does the design fit into only 1120 LUTs on ForgeFPGA?"

Answer: "By using an $O(1)$ running-sum filter with bit-shift division and an accumulator-based peak detector, our entire ShrikeFi design consumes just 195 LUT5s (17.41%) and 110 flip-flops, leaving over 82% of the chip free."
